Product Design

Continuing Community

Bringing BRIDGEGOOD alumni back as mentors

BRIDGEGOOD is an Oakland nonprofit that opens design careers to job seekers. In a three-week sprint, we designed a product that connects its alumni with new designers.

Role
Lead Product Designer
Team
3 designers
Timeline
3-week design sprint
Tool
Adobe XD

Problem

Design a new, responsive product for BRIDGEGOOD in one three-week Google design sprint.

Solution

Fireside chats between industry pros and new designers.

01 · Stakeholders

An open brief. Start with stakeholders.

With no defined problem, alignment came first. I interviewed three stakeholders and mapped their long-term goals.

Innovation

Distinct from the BRIDGEGOOD brand.

Scalable impact

Open to all. Able to grow nationally.

Low maintenance

Easy to run.

The insight that set our audience

Thousands of students have come through BRIDGEGOOD. Few come back to help the next ones.

02 · Research

Two groups, one gap to bridge.

We talked to alumni, and to career switchers chasing their first UX job.

  • Career switchersDifficulty landing their first job
  • AlumniWanting to help, but not knowing where to start
  • AlumniInsecurity about being good enough to mentor
  • Career switchersA lack of visibility into current industry needs

03 · Market

Six competitors, compared.

I compared six platforms related to the UX/educational space. Because this product was specific to BRIDGEGOOD and it's community, I didn't necessarily want to find differentiators, but more so gather inspiration to integrate into our solution. 

04 · Structure

Scope the MVP.

Challenge

Our first site map had two dashboards, solving for each persona individually. With three weeks left, we needed to simplify our MVP.

Solution

Build for students first. They benefit right away, and mentors join by hosting chats.

05 · Design system

Built on the existing brand.

The system keeps BRIDGEGOOD’s color and type, and adds components that fit.

06 · Final product

The finished product.

Browse chats, sign up, and join a video call with a mentor, on desktop, tablet and mobile. Two rounds of testing shaped it; the notes below show what we learned and what changed.

Prototype walkthrough.

Product page

The app at a glance.

Bay Area mentors, front and center.

What changed

Testing showed mentors were hard to browse in a horizontal scroll. A grid cut the time it took to connect.

Camps

Your dashboard of upcoming chats.

Fireside copy and visuals, with a ticket concept.

What changed

Renamed from “Your Schedule.” The fireside name gave the product its own identity inside BRIDGEGOOD’s brand.

Ignite

Browse topics. Sign up for chats.

Host and topic details help people come prepared.

What changed

Renamed from “Upcoming Tea Times.” Testing pushed the product toward casual chats with mentors, and a side profile made each host clearer.

Personalized form

Tells mentors who’s coming and what they care about.

Video chat

Industry knowledge to help break into UX.

Ask questions. Network. Sharpen skills.
