I learned about the power of designed experiences when I worked in hospitality.
I fell in love with crafting them when I saw them as one perfectly orchestrated system.

Danny Meyer calls it enlightened hospitality: the technical delivery of a product married to how it makes someone feel. As a waitress, I saw how each detail was an intentional choice to create a cohesive, subtly designed experience. I fell in love with those details and the delight they brought, admiring the larger system behind them and the effort it took to make it all seem so effortless.

My career has been guided by these details — a strong curiosity to learn and understand how they connect to one another, the larger ecosystem they make up, and what they mean for the people using them.

Design Philosophy

  • Design is a service

    Whether I’m designing an interface, a process, or model behavior, I'm always thinking about context — why is the user here? how do they behave? what visible and invisible factors play a part in their experience? Understanding the relation among these elements equips me with the ability to understand their output in forms of experience.

  • Move towards the unknown

    What scares me: getting stuck, playing it safe, comfort over growth.
    What doesn't scare me: change, carving a path, learning how to adapt.
    Uncertainty and ambiguity are the greatest catalysts to growth, and when they show up in my work, all I see is "opportunity."

  • Know the details to know the system

    Why? How? When? Who? What? Where? And then? I start with these seven questions to help me dig into a problem I don't understand yet. Every person I talk to, process I look at, tool I become aware of, helps me understand the root of the issue and what parts of it are the stickiest. Only then, once I understand the details, to then understand the system, can I find solutions that work.
