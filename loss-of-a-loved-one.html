Service Design

Loss of a Loved One

How families close a member’s accounts, redesigned.

Closing a member’s accounts took up to 42 days, mostly by mail. Grieving families moved their money elsewhere. I mapped the process and set a phased path to self-service.

Role
Sr Service Designer
Partners
DAS processing team, member service reps, Marketing, executive leadership
Methods
Journey mapping, interviews, complaint and survey analysis, call listening, competitive review
Timeline
April – September 2025

Problem

The process for handling deceased accounts is filled with operational inefficiencies that overwhelm families during an already stressful period of grief.

Solution

Clarify process steps online, update outdated trust documents, and work towards digitizing document uploads. 

01 · Research

Internal mapping married with existing member feedback.

Instead of directly surveying and speaking with grieving beneficiaries, I triangulated complaint data, existing survey responses, and recorded calls to form an understanding of the service experience. I married this data with internal stakeholder interviews to embed backend processes into the map.

Stakeholder interviews Complaint data Survey responses Call listening

02 · Insights

Reliance on a mailing system, missing documents, and no timeline transparency compound member stress.

Reliance on mail

Documents could only be mailed in, adding 16–20 days.

Outdated letters

Letters had errors, and were often addressed to the deceased.

No transparency

No timeline. Unclear requirements. Little contact.

Up to 42 days to close an account. Mostly due to reliance on mail.

Despite no law requirement for documents to be sent and received by mail, it was the only option for beneficiaries. 

Forms failed to outline the correct documents needed, making beneficiaries restart the process and adding to an already lengthy timeline.

03 · Decisions

Two phases, dependent on what operations could take on.

Phase 1 · Quick wins

Make the deceased webpage easy to find and follow

A high-fidelity mockup, based on competitive research, adopted and launched by the Marketing team.

Phase 2 · Heavy lift

A digital, self-service path for beneficiaries

Parallel processing, instant uploads and letters, proactive updates, a faster timeline.

04 · Design

Redesigning the webpage with competitor references.

About half of peers offered online self-service options and a guide or a short outline of the process. The new page leads with four steps, three ways to notify us, and the documents people need.
